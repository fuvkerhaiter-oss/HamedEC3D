## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

WEGO SHIIIP is **not a SaaS**. It is a **single-merchant B2C e-commerce store** (dropshipping, pet products in practice) with a walkable 3D supermarket front-end, plus a small **side product that rents out 4 virtual shop units** inside that 3D store to other brands. The only recurring-revenue element is that 4-unit rental, with a hard ceiling of about €116/month at regular prices.

**The engineering is unusually good for a 7-day-old solo project.** The first commit was 2026-11-20. Since then there are 43 commits, ~40,000 lines of TypeScript across 341 files, 16 SQL migrations, 60 tables, 25 API routes, 30 admin pages and a seller panel. Typecheck and lint are clean, 249/249 unit tests pass, and `npm audit` finds 0 vulnerabilities [TESTED]. The payment and order core is built with care you rarely see at this stage: server-side pricing, a DB-enforced order state machine, webhook signature checks plus re-verification with Stripe, amount matching, idempotency, and RLS on all 60 tables.

**Commercially it is at zero, and today it cannot take a single order.**
- On the live site (www.wegoshiiip.com), checkout refuses every destination, including Italy, the home market: "We can't ship to IT / DE / FR / US yet" [TESTED].
- 3 of the 4 live products carry a "SAMPLE PRODUCT" badge [TESTED].
- The live Terms of Sale, Privacy Policy and Returns pages still show `[LEGAL COMPANY NAME]`, `[VAT NUMBER]`, `[REGISTERED ADDRESS]`, `[REA NUMBER]` [TESTED]. For an Italian seller that is a legal defect, not just a cosmetic one.
- All 4 rental units show "Available": 0 tenants [TESTED].

The product's central bet is that people want to walk through a 3D supermarket to buy dropshipped pet gadgets. That bet is **unvalidated**, and the wider market evidence (metaverse retail, virtual malls) points against it as a primary shopping channel.

**Bottom line:** strong technical prototype, pre-launch, pre-revenue, no demonstrated demand. Its value today is mostly the code and the founder's demonstrated speed. It is not yet a business.

## 2. FINAL VERDICT

**What it is:**
- A well-engineered, feature-rich e-commerce prototype with a distinctive 3D storefront.
- Correct handling of money, payments and order state.
- A large admin back office.
- Deployed on Vercel + Supabase.

**What it is not:**
- A SaaS: there are no tenants, subscriptions or multi-tenant product to sell. Shop rental is a 4-slot feature, not a platform.
- A functioning store: no shipping zones are configured, so orders are impossible.
- Legally ready to sell in the EU: placeholder seller identity, and a strike-through "regular price" that was never charged.
- Validated by any customer, traffic or revenue data: none was available to me.

## 5. WHAT IS ACTUALLY GOOD

1. **Payment and order integrity** [CODE]: `lib/payments/webhook.ts`.
   - The webhook verifies the signature, dedupes the event, re-fetches the PaymentIntent from Stripe, checks it succeeded, checks the provider amount against the event, then the event amount against the order total. Only after that does it transition through `transitionOrder`.
   - Everything runs in one DB transaction, so a failure rolls back the dedupe record and Stripe retries.
   - Late payments on cancelled orders are recorded and flagged instead of lost. Disputes put supplier purchasing on hold.
   - This is better than many funded startups.
2. **Server-authoritative pricing** [CODE/DOC, e2e asserts it]: the client sends only `{variantId, quantity}`, and money is handled as integer minor units.
3. **Database defence in depth** [CODE]: RLS enabled on all 60 tables (checked migration by migration). DB triggers guard order transitions, immutable order amounts and the fulfilment gate. Supplier credentials are never readable by clients.
4. **Admin auth** [CODE]:
   - Separate admin identity store with scrypt hashing.
   - Session tokens stored only as SHA-256 hashes.
   - `__Host-` cookie with `SameSite=Strict`, 12 h TTL.
   - Lockout, dummy-hash timing equalisation.
   - Every one of 80 admin/seller server actions goes through a permission-checking wrapper (counted per file).
5. **Security headers** [TESTED on prod]: CSP, HSTS with preload, X-Frame-Options DENY, nosniff, COOP, a restrictive Permissions-Policy. There is also an Origin-based CSRF guard on all state-changing requests.
6. **Test discipline**: 249 unit tests, integration tests on a real Postgres, SQL-level RLS tests, Playwright. Typecheck and lint are clean [TESTED].
7. **EU consumer-law features**: an online withdrawal button (CRD art. 11a), 14-day window logic, signed order links [CODE]. Few small stores have this.
8. **Execution speed**: the breadth delivered in one week is exceptional [TESTED via git log].
9. **The 3D store works in production** [TESTED]: it loads, renders, and offers a mobile touch mode.

1. **Production can't sell** [TESTED]: no shipping zones, so every country gets a 422.
2. **Legal identity placeholders are live** on Terms, Privacy and Returns [TESTED]. The checkout's "legal pages published" gate (`lib/checkout/service.ts` `legalBlocker`) checks only that the pages are published, not that they are filled in.
3. **Sample/mock products are live** next to the one real product [TESTED].
4. **Scope far exceeds traction**:
   - Already built: multiplayer rooms, proximity voice chat, bots, a lucky wheel, Christmas decor and music, minimap, rentals, a seller panel, a dropshipping automation engine, a visitor log, and 30 admin screens.
   - Missing: the first sale. Most of these features have no proven user value.
5. **Positioning is incoherent** [TESTED]: supermarket departments ("Pantry, Snacks, Drinks") with pet gadgets inside (a cat laser listed under Pantry). The tagline is "Happy Family, Happy Animals".
6. **Rental pricing framing** [TESTED]: "Regular price €29 → €20, −31%", while no rental has ever sold at €29. Under the EU Omnibus Directive / Italian Consumer Code art. 17-bis, the reference price must be the lowest price applied in the previous 30 days, so this framing is likely non-compliant.

8. **No CI, no error monitoring, no product analytics** [CODE: no `.github/`, no Sentry/PostHog]. A custom visitor log stands in for analytics.
9. **IP/licensing exposure** [TESTED/CODE]:

10. **Repo hygiene**: ~40 MB of raw art, FBX files and an MP3 are committed at the repo root (`.git` is 74 MB).
11. **Hosting**: the production function region is `iad1` (US-East) while the business and most customers are in the EU [TESTED: `x-vercel-id: cdg1::iad1`]. That adds latency, and is a data-location question for GDPR (the Supabase region is NOT VERIFIED).

## 9. TECHNICAL AUDIT

**Stack.** Next.js 16 App Router, React 19, TypeScript strict, Tailwind 4, R3F/three 0.187, zustand and zod on the front end. Server side: `postgres` (direct SQL) plus the Supabase SDK, Vercel, and Resend. Dependencies are minimal: 12 runtime deps, 0 known vulnerabilities [TESTED].

**Strengths.**
- Pure business rules (pricing, state machine, shipping quote, coupons, FX) are isolated and unit-tested.
- Services take a `Db` handle, so they compose inside transactions.
- Invariants are enforced twice, in TS and in SQL triggers.
- An email outbox and a fulfilment job queue use cron as a backstop.
- An env checker (`check:env`) refuses unsafe production configurations.

**Weaknesses.**
- `src/lib/rate-limit.ts` is an in-process `Map`. On Vercel each function instance has its own memory and instances are recycled, so limits on checkout, OTP, contact and quote are largely ineffective against a distributed or even moderately persistent client. The code's own comment admits this.
- The visitor beacon writes to Postgres on every page view plus a once-per-minute ping per open tab [CODE `api/visit`]. At scale that is the dominant write load, for a feature a hosted analytics tool provides better.
- Unpaid orders reserve stock for 25 h (`PENDING_ORDER_TTL_HOURS`). Anonymous checkouts can therefore lock finite stock (see §10).
- There is no CI. Correctness depends on someone running tests locally.
- Two stores of state: the SQL trigger logic plus the TS state machine. That is correct, but every change needs both.
- Repo contains large binaries; the 3D store ships ~11.7 MB decoded assets including seasonal models [TESTED].

**Load scenarios** [EST — architectural reasoning, not benchmarks]:
- **100 active users:** no issues. Pages are edge-cached; the 3D assets come from the CDN.
- **1,000:** fine for pages. The visitor beacon generates ~1,000 writes/min. Multiplayer is ~100 rooms of 10 on Supabase Realtime, which is within paid-tier limits. P2P voice without TURN will fail for a share of users behind strict NATs.
- **10,000:** Postgres write load from beacons (~10k/min) plus Realtime presence/broadcast message volume become the cost and capacity drivers. You would need the Supabase Pro/Team tier and a pooler; at that point the per-instance rate limiter is useless.
- **100,000:** needs real analytics instead of DB beacons, a dedicated realtime/SFU service for voice, an edge WAF, and probably a multi-region setup. The commerce core (orders) would be fine; order volume, not visitor volume, is what it was designed for.

## 12. COMPETITOR ANALYSIS

Your product sits in two markets at once. Competitors are categorised by which half they compete with.

**A. As a pet-products online store (what customers actually buy)**
- **Market leaders:** Amazon, Chewy (US), Zooplus (EU), Fressnapf/Maxi Zoo (EU). They win on range, price, delivery speed and trust; you cannot compete on any of these with dropshipped goods.
- **Direct competitors:** thousands of Shopify dropship pet stores selling the same supplier SKUs (cat fountains, laser toys, paw cleaners). Vendor guides describe the niche as crowded on obvious items and recommend sub-niches ([CJ Dropshipping](https://www.cjdropshipping.com/blogs/dropshipping-niches/Best-Dropshipping-Pet-Products), [Dropified](https://www.dropified.com/blog/pet-products-dropshipping-in-2026-best-selling-items-suppliers-profit-margins/), [Debutify](https://www.debutify.com/blog/dropshipping-pet-products-with-profitable-niches-winning-items-in-2026)). Market size figures in these sources vary by ~2× and are vendor-published: treat them as rough.
- **Your differentiation here:** only the 3D experience. Users will choose Amazon/Chewy/Zooplus for faster delivery, reviews and returns they trust.

**B. As 3D virtual-store technology / virtual mall (the rental side)**
- **Obsess:** the enterprise 3D virtual stores leader; Shopify listing at $3,000/month ([Shopify App Store](https://apps.shopify.com/obsessvr-experience), [Retail Dive](https://www.retaildive.com/news/ava-by-obsess-metaverse-virtual-store-builder/646987)). Brands get their *own* branded store, not a unit in someone else's mall.
- **3D XR Store (Imersive.io):** a Shopify app with walkable 3D aisles in the browser, from about $19 to $499/month ([Shopify](https://apps.shopify.com/3d-store)). This is the closest functional analogue, and it's sold to merchants as a tool, which is a better business model than yours.
- **Odyssey (Exarta), Bazaar VR:** free/low-cost Shopify 3D store embeds ([Odyssey](https://apps.shopify.com/odyssey), [Bazaar VR](https://apps.shopify.com/bazaar-vr)).
- **Emperia, Napster Immersive:** enterprise browser-based virtual storefronts ([Mixed News on Emperia](https://mixed-news.com/en/emperias-virtual-store-fronts-preview-the-future-of-ecommerce/), [Napster on Shopify](https://apps.shopify.com/napster-experience)).
- **Virtual malls (Decentraland districts, Roblox brand malls):** the closest to "rent a shop in our 3D mall". 2026 coverage questions whether virtual pop-ups still produce business results ([The Storefront](https://www.thestorefront.com/go/virtual-reality-stores-retail)), and the mall model largely faded after 2022.

**Comparison.** A brand renting your €20/month unit gets visibility only to *your* traffic, which is currently ~0. For $19/month it could have its own 3D store on its own Shopify site (3D XR Store), or a free one (Odyssey/Bazaar). **There is no reason today for a brand to rent from you** unless you bring audience. Your rental product is therefore an audience business, not a technology business.

## 14. BUSINESS MODEL

**Current revenue lines:**
- **Retail margin on dropshipped goods**: €16–32 tickets [TESTED]. Typical dropship gross margin is 20–40% before ads [EST]. After Stripe (~1.5% + €0.25 EU cards [EST]), paid acquisition (often €10–30 CAC per order in pet [EST]) and returns, low-ticket dropshipping is frequently **contribution-negative** without organic traffic.
- **Shop rentals**: 4 units × €29 max ≈ **€116/month ceiling**, €12.50–€20/month at launch prices. Even fully sold out this would not cover hosting plus your time.

**Not present:** subscriptions, SaaS tiers, upsells, a free plan or trial, retention mechanics (accounts exist; no loyalty/email marketing flows seen).

**Costs** [EST]: Vercel Pro ~$20/user/month; Supabase Pro ~$25/month+, which Realtime/visitor-beacon volume grows; Resend free→$20; Stripe per-transaction. Small at today's volume.

**Recommended pricing direction** (only if you pivot to selling the tech):
- Benchmarks: Shopify 3D apps cluster at **$0–$499/month**, enterprise ~$3,000/month.
- A credible self-serve offer would be ~$29–$99/month for "embed a branded walkable 3D store on your Shopify/website".
- Do not pursue this until you can show *any* conversion uplift data.

**Proven performance:** none. No revenue, customers, conversion, churn or CAC data was available. All figures above are theoretical.

## 19. FINAL HONEST OPINION

- **Is this SaaS genuinely good?** It is not a SaaS. As software it is genuinely good. As a business it does not exist yet.
- **Better than the average solo-dev project?** Yes, clearly, on engineering rigour: payments, RLS, tests, legal-flow awareness. Most solo projects never get this right.
- **Good enough to compete?** Not today. As a pet store it loses to Amazon/Chewy/Zooplus and to thousands of identical dropship stores. As 3D tech it loses to $0–$19/month Shopify apps that merchants install on their own sites.
- **Genuine chance of making money?** Small but real *if* the 3D store proves to be a traffic hook (social virality) for a narrow pet niche. Close to none for the rental line in its current form.
- **Worth selling now?** No. You would get low four figures. It is worth more to you as a launchpad or a portfolio piece.
- **Biggest weakness:** zero demand validation, and production can't take an order.
- **Biggest strength:** execution speed with a solid commerce core.
- **Would I invest?** No. There is no traction, positioning is unclear, the core assumption is unproven, the niche is saturated and there is no moat. I would revisit after ~€5–10k of revenue with a measured 3D conversion effect.
- **What I'd do first:** make checkout work in Italy and 2–3 EU countries, fix the legal identity, clean the catalog, then put €300 of ads behind it and measure.
- **What I would NOT waste time on:** more 3D features (seasonal decor, bots, wheel, minimap upgrades, more rental units), more admin screens, multi-currency/tax edge cases for markets with no orders, and SaaS-ifying the rental system before anyone has rented a unit.
